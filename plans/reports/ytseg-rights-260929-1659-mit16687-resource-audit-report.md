# YTSeg rights audit: MIT 16.687, seven locked TRAIN videos

**Checked:** 2026-09-29, Asia/Bangkok. **Scope:** rights evidence for local, noncommercial RQ1 research processing and separately for redistribution of original video, frames, subtitles, and derivatives. This is a source and component audit, not legal clearance or D2 approval. All seven IDs are one course/lecturer group, so at most **one independent group**.

## Decision

**Rank 1 — retain MIT 16.687 for private research qualification.** The current [MIT OCW terms](https://ocw.mit.edu/pages/privacy-and-terms-of-use/) expressly allow OCW content for AI training/development/improvement unless otherwise specified, subject to attribution, noncommercial use and ShareAlike. The [course page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-687-private-pilot-ground-school-january-iap-2019/) identifies the instructors and lecture videos; MIT's [course download page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-687-private-pilot-ground-school-january-iap-2019/download/) intentionally offers video files. The [Internet Archive item](https://archive.org/details/MIT16.687IAP19), uploaded by MIT-OCW, labels the course CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. These are positive **publisher-level** grounds for local noncommercial analysis, stronger than mere public availability. Attribution and use restrictions must be documented in the protocol. The study's actual intended use and output distribution need to meet these terms.

**Do not promote all seven to unqualified `pass_rights` yet.** The local **source MP4s** carry a CC BY-NC-SA **3.0** notice in `copyright`/`comment`, whereas current OCW terms and Archive item say **4.0**. Official slide PDFs for **six of the seven** explicitly label some third-party assets as excluded from the OCW Creative Commons license; the seventh (Lecture 16) labels several assets Public Domain but its PDF alone cannot establish every frame of the video. The terms say OCW reviewed ownership and obtained licenses to publish; that establishes a publisher due-diligence claim, not that excluded components are sublicensed for the proposed research or redistribution use. Thus mark **publisher-level research permission evidenced; full-video research scope `hold_component_scope`**, and **redistribution `hold_component_and_version_scope`**. This is neither a finding that research is forbidden nor that redistribution is permitted.

**Rank 2 — publish only source links, hashes, IDs and aggregate metrics for now.** This has low adoption risk and does not require copying the MP4, caption or image. The seven local media receipts already support technical/media qualification. **Rank 3 — distribute video, extracted frames or captions** only after resolving component permissions, license-version/attribution obligations and any identifiable-person limits. It offers easier reproducibility but highest rights cost. There is no need to change dataset, add a fingerprint gate, select a model, or run official RQ1 to make this rights decision.

| Output choice | Research utility | Rights uncertainty | Maintenance / cost | Rank |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| Private local analysis, no source assets in artifacts | Full multimodal evaluation; conditional on intended noncommercial use | Per-component training/research scope still needs check | Maintain source/credit ledger; low distribution cost | 1 |
| IDs, URLs, hashes, protocol, aggregate metrics | Reproducible acquisition; no copy of source media | Low; check short quoted text separately | Low | 2 |
| Redistribute MP4, frames, subtitles | Convenient replay | High: mixed assets, people, CC 3.0/4.0 tension | Asset-level clearance and ShareAlike obligations | 3 |

## Source credibility and limits

- **MIT OCW course/resource/download pages, seven slide PDFs and terms:** first-party, highest weight for publisher identity, offered download, explicit exclusions, and general policy. Current terms last updated 2026-08-11, later than the 2019/2020 recordings; applicability to older media/embedded 3.0 notice merits explicit protocol treatment.
- **MIT-OCW Internet Archive item:** publisher-uploaded mirror and independent delivery system, strong for source provenance and item-level 4.0 metadata, not an independent rights-holder clearance of each component.
- **MP4 embedded metadata and decoded frames:** direct file evidence, strongest for the 3.0 notice and sampled content, weak for ownership or permission. Three frames/video cannot establish absence of other third-party assets.
- **Current MIT terms' AI clause:** authorizes qualifying OCW content for AI model work, with conditions; does not prove this project's exact activity, future commercialization, release format, or every embedded third-party asset satisfies them. No author was contacted and no legal exception was assumed.

## Next actions

1. State exact intended use in draft protocol: local noncommercial research, attribution for MIT/creators/links, no copied media/frames/subtitles in public artifacts, and model/output ShareAlike handling if applicable. Keep publisher-level permission separate from unresolved full-component scope.
2. Map the PDFs' now-identified excluded components to video timestamps. Start with Lecture 16 because its PDF showed no explicit exclusions and multiple Public Domain notices; inspect the whole video for extra components before considering promotion. For the six with explicit exclusions, record the affected spans and whether a research use other than CC reuse is supported. This narrows holds per video without inventing a new gate.
3. Resolve 3.0-in-file versus current 4.0-on-site/Archive for the intended use. If ambiguity remains material, ask MIT OCW a scoped question **only with the user's separate authorization to contact them**; document their answer. Do not infer 4.0 relicensing from current page alone.
